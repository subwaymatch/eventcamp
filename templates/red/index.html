Speakers

Featuring the best innovators

Eight talks, one track, nothing running in parallel to choose between. Every speaker below has shipped the thing they are talking about, so this is an account of what worked and what it cost — not a survey of what might.

Priya Raghunathan Founder, Eventcamp

Started Eventcamp in 2019, after a decade of running platform teams. Talks about the parts of a migration nobody writes the blog post about.

Leah Kim Co-founder, Fieldnote

Builds offline-first tools for field researchers. Has opinions about sync conflicts that she came by the hard way.

Tomas Brandt Design Lead, Northmark

Works the seam between a design system and the code that consumes it, and spends most of his time convincing both sides it is one job.

Topics

The topics being covered include the following:

  • Deploying to the edge: what genuinely gets faster, what gets harder, and the request patterns that make cache invalidation worth the trouble.

  • Rendering models measured against each other — server components, islands and plain progressive enhancement, on one page rather than three demos.

  • Real-time collaboration without reaching for a CRDT library, and recognising the point where you have to.

  • Search people can actually use: ranking, typo tolerance, and why relevance is a product problem before it is an infrastructure one.

  • Documentation as an engineering artefact: generated reference, hand-written guides, and keeping the two from drifting apart.

  • Types from the database column through to the form field, and the places along the way where they stop paying for themselves.

  • Design systems after version one — deprecation, migration, and the long tail of components nobody will let you delete.

  • Internationalisation past the string table: dates, sorting, bidirectional text, and the layouts that break when German arrives.

News

From the Chicago Weekly

The conference “Modern Web Development Techniques” will take place from 10:00 at the Hotel Energy in Chicago, IL on 27 March 2026.

Tickets went on sale in December and the hall holds 400 — about half what the organisers reckon they could sell. “We get asked to move to a bigger room every year,” says Priya Raghunathan, who founded the conference. “One room is the whole idea.”

Talks run 35 minutes with ten for questions, taken from the floor rather than through an app. Recordings go up free about six weeks later.

A quarter of the tickets are held back for a grant covering entry, travel within the Midwest and a night at the hotel. Applications close on 6 February and are read with the names removed.

The Hotel Energy is ten minutes from the Blue Line and holds the conference room rate until the end of February. Lunch is included, and the kitchen works from the dietary requirements on the registration form rather than a show of hands on the day.

There is no exhibition floor. Sponsors get their name on the site and on the lanyard and nothing else — a decision the organisers call the least popular and most correct one they have made.
